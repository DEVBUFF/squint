# Branding & positioning — trends · October 2026

## TL;DR
- **Evolve, don't erase.** The big 2026 rebrands (Coca-Cola, KFC, Instagram, McLaren, Poetry Foundation) amplified assets people already recognised. Equity-erasing resets got punished, most visibly Cracker Barrel's logo reversal in 2025. [13][16][17][18][21][70]
- **AI is why companies rebrand, not what they say.** AI pivots are driving masterbrand consolidation and renames (Intuit, Calendly, Pleo, Freepik→Magnific). Meanwhile 50% of US consumers prefer brands that avoid GenAI in consumer-facing content, so lead with outcomes, not "AI-powered". [22][24][26][27][35][36]
- **Proof-of-human is a brand asset.** Examples: film-shot campaigns, hand-made idents, process content, and imperfect "just-not-quite-right" craft (Canva calls 2026 "Imperfect by Design"). [9][11][12][33]
- **Custom type is now the identity's backbone.** Nearly every major 2026 system shipped a bespoke family, often with mono or handwriting cuts. [17][22][26][54]
- **Identities are time-based.** LogoLounge's 2026 report is "all on the move", and sonic logos are becoming sonic systems. [6][30][31]
- **AI agents get faces.** Meta's Jolly and OpenAI's dots launched as cute characters in September 2026 and immediately drew scrutiny. [40][41]
- **App stores reward positioning by intent.** That means up to 70 keyword-matched App Store custom product pages and one-click keyword custom store listings on Google Play. [64][65][66]

## Baseline (expected now, not a trend)
- **Multi-mode logo and icon kit** — full lockup, compact wordmark, a glyph legible at 16 px, and an app icon built in layers for Apple's light, dark, tinted and transparent glass modes. Remove baked-in shadows and bevels, and avoid thin lines, which disappear in glass. [63]
- **At least one ownable non-logo asset** — a colour, shape, character or sound that can carry the brand on its own (Coca-Cola's Dynamic Ribbon and Spencerian script, KFC's bucket). Rebrands are now judged on how well they use these. [13][16]
- **Positioning written before visuals** — competitive alternatives, distinct capabilities, differentiated value, best-fit customers, market category. Agree it cross-functionally and re-check it on a fixed cadence (Dunford recommends every 6 months), not every model release. [57][59][60]
- **Logo motion behaviour defined** — intro, loader and transition states for app and video. Static-only identities now look unfinished next to systems that "flex, pulse, rotate". [6]
- **Transparent generative-AI use** — don't ship undisclosed AI hero imagery in consumer work. 68% of US consumers frequently wonder whether the content they see is real. [35][39]
- **Store creative = real UI + outcome copy** — the opening seconds of the preview video and the first screenshots carry conversion, and metadata, creatives and product should stay aligned. [65]

## Trends

### brand-evolve-not-erase — Evolve, don't erase: doubling down on distinctive assets
- **Status:** mainstream
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Flagship 2025–26 rebrands refine and systematise what people already recognise instead of replacing it. In a polarised climate, wholesale simplification draws instant backlash, and LogoLounge and Creative Bloq called 2025 the most contentious year for rebrands.
- **Seen in:**
  - Coca-Cola global identity (Jul 2026). Script, Dynamic Ribbon and Arden Square retained; the can wordmark is vertical again.
  - KFC (Jun 2026, JKR). Bucket as framing system; Creative Review called it "doubling down".
  - Instagram (Aug 2026). First refresh in 10+ years: bolder semi-script, gradient used more subtly.
  - McLaren (Sep 2026). Archive-derived wordmark; Speedmark, papaya and Speedy Kiwi kept; some fans called it bland.
  - Poetry Foundation and Poetry magazine (announced Sep 28, 2026, Pentagram). Reworked Pegasus, wider palette, bolder type.
  - Counter-example: Cracker Barrel (Aug 2025) dropped its "Old Timer" and reverted within about a week. YouGov: 76% of aware respondents preferred the old logo. Fast Company's designer panel still listed it among 2025's most noteworthy rebrands, "for better or worse".
  - Brand New's 2026 "big stories" are mostly evolutions of this kind (Coca-Cola, KFC, Intuit, Threads, Sam's Club).
  - [13][16][17][18][21][7][8][23][70]
- **Fits:** any brand with 3+ years of recognition, consumer apps with a known icon, scale-ups that are refreshing rather than pivoting.
- **Avoid when:** the old equity is actively toxic or misleading. Also when the business has genuinely changed category, though even then keep one bridge asset (Bentley modernised its Winged B "without pulling a Jaguar"). [20]
- **Implement:** Run a distinctive-asset audit (recognition × uniqueness) before any sketching. Keep the top 1–2 assets and redraw them for screens: thicker strokes, simplified details, motion-ready. Make the refresh about the system (type, layout, motion) more than the mark. Pre-test with existing users, and stage the rollout across touchpoints.
- **Sources:** [13], [16], [17], [18], [21], [7], [8], [23], [20], [70]

### brand-masterbrand-platform — Masterbrand consolidation & renames for the AI-platform era
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** As point products bolt on AI and expand, companies fold sub-brands into one parent system, or rename outright, so they read as a single platform. The AI layer often gets its own named sub-identity.
- **Seen in:**
  - Intuit (Sep 2026, JKR). "One Intuit" system, custom typeface Tally, a short "i" glyph, and "Intuit Intelligence" pairing AI with human experts.
  - Calendly (Aug 2026, Smith & Diction). "Flex-bubbles" system for a five-product family, neon palette swapped for pastels, AI assistant Callie.
  - Pleo (Sep 2026, Porto Rocha). From expense tool to "spend platform".
  - Freepik → Magnific (Apr 2026). Renamed as an "AI creative platform".
  - Amazon (Apr 2025, Koto). 50+ sub-brands unified around "Smile Orange".
  - [22][47][24][25][26][27][19]
- **Fits:** multi-product SaaS, fintech and creative-tool companies whose name describes a legacy single feature.
- **Avoid when:** the sub-brands have stronger equity than the parent. Skydance's house-of-brands approach after acquiring WBD keeps CNN and HBO front and centre. [48]
- **Implement:** Map the architecture first (masterbrand, endorsed or house of brands). Give products a shared shape or icon grammar plus one colour each. Name AI capabilities as an extension of the parent ("<Brand> Intelligence"), not a separate mascot brand, unless it is consumer-facing. If renaming, pick a name with room to expand into new media types, and keep old URLs and APIs running in parallel; Magnific kept the legacy endpoints for 6+ months.
- **Sources:** [22], [47], [24], [25], [26], [27], [19], [48]

### brand-custom-type-families — Custom type families as the core identity asset
- **Status:** mainstream
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** A bespoke type family now does most of the identity work. These families often ship with extra cuts (mono, handwriting, display) so the brand stays recognisable when the logo is absent, especially inside product UI and AI-generated layouts.
- **Seen in:**
  - Instagram (2026): updated Instagram Sans plus new Instagram Pen and Instagram Mono.
  - Intuit (2026): Tally.
  - Pleo (2026): Pleo Scandi Gellix, for marketing and product.
  - KFC (2026): bespoke typefaces.
  - Threads (2026): moved off Instagram Sans onto its own "Trending Chisel" type base.
  - OpenAI (2025): OpenAI Sans by ABC Dinamo, with a mono version.
  - Arsenal (Oct 2026): Rocastle blackletter by David Rudnick.
  - [17][22][26][16][28][29][54]
- **Fits:** scale-ups and anything type-led (fintech, AI, media, dev tools). For seed-stage startups, an exclusive or less-used retail family plus custom logo lettering gets most of the benefit.
- **Avoid when:** budget forces a half-finished family (poor language support, no UI weights). Also avoid expressive display faces as UI text.
- **Implement:** Brief a family, not a font: a display cut for brand moments, a text and UI cut tuned for small sizes, and optionally mono for data and code. Commission character in only 3–5 signature glyphs (as Instagram did with "s", "r", "g"). Ship it as a variable font with full script coverage for target markets. Build the logo from the same DNA.
- **Sources:** [17], [22], [26], [16], [28], [29], [54]

### brand-motion-sound-native — Motion- and sound-native identity systems
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Marks are designed to move and sound from day one. LogoLounge's 2026 report, drawn from 30,000+ logos, makes motion its overarching theme (trends like Slant Break, Phasers, Roll Over, Breezy). Sonic branding is shifting from one-off jingles to modular "sonic systems", with an emphasis on human-performed sound.
- **Seen in:**
  - Apple TV (Nov 2025). Hand-made glass logo animation plus a mnemonic composed by Finneas.
  - OpenAI (2025). A pulsing "voice" disc developed with Studio Dumbar.
  - Domino's (Oct 2025). Tagline doubles as jingle and logo.
  - Crompton (Sep 2026). Master brand plus sonic branding system.
  - Netflix "tudum", extended across live events and physical spaces.
  - [30][11][29][32][43][31]
- **Fits:** consumer apps (launch and loading states), AI voice products, media, gaming, fintech with notification sounds.
- **Avoid when:** motion hurts accessibility (respect reduced-motion), or the sound would play in quiet B2B contexts uninvited.
- **Implement:**
  - Write motion principles (easing, rhythm, a signature transition) and one 1–3 s logo animation that doubles as the app loader.
  - Spec three sonic lengths: a ~1 s UI sting, a ~5 s ident and a long cut.
  - Prefer real instruments or recorded sources over stock synths, and test against emotional attributes before rollout.
- **Sources:** [6], [30], [11], [29], [32], [43], [31]

### brand-proof-of-human — Proof-of-human craft signals
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** With trust in AI content falling, brands deliberately show human authorship: film photography, practical effects, "human-made" claims, and behind-the-scenes process content. It's Nice That frames showing your workings as the new authenticity proof. At least eight competing "human-made" label schemes exist, and none is standard yet.
- **Seen in:**
  - ChatGPT's first brand campaign (Sep 2025): "Every frame was shot on film."
  - Apple TV ident (2025): glass logo made by hand, without CGI.
  - Heineken, Polaroid and Cadbury "human-made" campaigns (2025).
  - Apple's *Pluribus* credits: "Made by Humans".
  - Counter-example: McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas ad within days (Dec 2025).
  - [33][11][37][39]
- **Fits:** consumer brands, creative tools, premium, and ironically AI companies, which most need to look human-led.
- **Avoid when:** the claim isn't true end-to-end (backlash risk), or the product itself is AI-generated content. Then be transparent rather than "human-washing".
- **Implement:** Make craft visible in the assets themselves: grain, real lenses, practical effects, real customers. Publish process (making-of reels, sketches, type-design stories). Write a clear GenAI use policy for brand imagery. Use "made by people" claims only with proof.
- **Sources:** [11], [33], [37], [38], [39], [35], [36]

### brand-tactile-imperfect — Tactile, imperfect, lo-fi visual language
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** The counter-move to AI polish is designed imperfection, which Wolff Olins' Forest Young calls "just-exactly-not-quite-right". In practice:
  - **Logos:** melting or "blotch" wordmarks.
  - **Type:** pick-and-mix lettersets.
  - **Print:** low-ink, photocopier textures.
  - **Imagery:** collage "visual index" layouts.
  - **Illustration:** hand-drawn, woodcut and painterly styles replace flat vector people.

  Maximalism and minimalism coexist. Canva reports lo-fi searches up 527% and zine-style layouts up 85%, but searches for "clean layouts / simple branding" also rose 54%.
- **Seen in:**
  - Kuda (Oct 2026, Barkas): hand-drawn illustrations and a custom Naira sign, moving past generic challenger-bank styling.
  - MorningStar Farms (Sep 2026): 1970s-style textures and type.
  - Vodafone Idea (Sep 2026): flat mark turned into a 3D sphere.
  - Untold (Sep 2026, The Working Assembly): painterly illustration, AI-assisted, which was debated.
  - [53][55][43][49][10][12]
- **Fits:** consumer apps, wellness, food, creative tools, emerging-market fintech, culture brands.
- **Avoid when:** enterprise security and finance buyers need precision cues. Texture must also never cost legibility or contrast. And "fake imperfection" generated by AI defeats the point.
- **Implement:** Keep the core system (grid, UI type) clean and put imperfection in a brand layer: hand-drawn accents, scanned textures, one irregular display face, analogue photography. In app icons, stay flat and frontal (Apple's guidance), and express tactility through motion and illustration instead.
- **Sources:** [9], [10], [12], [53], [55], [43], [49], [63]

### brand-technical-micrographics — Micrographics & monospace "technical" aesthetic
- **Status:** emerging
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Tiny technical text, labels, grids, timestamps and mono type are used as decorative texture, which It's Nice That calls "the aesthetics of technical information". In tech brands it pairs with mono cuts of brand typefaces and data-derived graphics.
- **Seen in:**
  - Instagram Mono (2026).
  - OpenAI Sans mono cut (2025).
  - Eluviant (2026, The Branx): Geist Mono and graphics built from optical hashes of surveillance images.
  - Pleo (2026): spreadsheet-inspired stripes.
  - Uncommon billboards with chemistry diagrams (2025–26).
  - [17][29][25][26][10]
- **Fits:** dev tools, AI infrastructure, data and security products, fintech for finance teams, scientific B2B.
- **Avoid when:** the audience is non-technical consumers who read micro-text as clutter. Also skip it where the decorative text could be mistaken for real data.
- **Implement:** Add a mono cut for numbers, labels and code. Derive graphic patterns from your real product data (spreadsheets, logs, telemetry) rather than stock "tech" visuals. Keep the micro-type decorative and non-essential for accessibility.
- **Sources:** [10], [17], [29], [25], [26]

### brand-ownable-signal-color — One ownable signal colour, off the category default
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Brands leave category colour codes (tech blue, fintech teal/grey, AI violet) for one saturated, ownable signal colour, usually on a calm neutral base.
- **Forecast backdrop:**
  - Pantone 2026: 11-4201 Cloud Dancer, a soft white and the first white Color of the Year since 1999.
  - WGSN × Coloro 2026: Transformative Teal (092-37-14). For 2027: Luminous Blue.
  - Pinterest Palette 2026: Persimmon, Cool Blue, Jade, Plum Noir, Wasabi. Plum Noir saves are the fastest-rising.
  - Pantone's 2027 Color of the Year is not yet announced (usually December). Its Spring/Summer 2027 NYFW forecast (Sep 2026) mixes bold reds, oranges and corals with pale herbal tones. [71]
- **Seen in:**
  - Samsara (Aug 2026, Base Design): "ditched tech blue for construction yellow".
  - Pleo (2026): a cooler, brighter pink against category greys and blues.
  - Magnific (2026): Magnific Magenta.
  - Amazon (2025): Smile Orange.
  - Calendly (2026): neon swapped for pastels around its core blue.
  - [51][26][25][19][1][2][3][4][5]
- **Fits:** challengers in crowded SaaS, fintech and AI categories, and B2B brands selling to non-office workers.
- **Avoid when:** the hue collides with the category leader or a regulated meaning (red or green in finance). Also when the accent can't pass contrast as text: put yellows and chartreuse on dark, not white. And don't adopt a Color of the Year literally; it dates fast.
- **Implement:**
  - Plot the top 10 competitors' primary hues and pick one far from the cluster.
  - Define a neutral base (warm off-white, Cloud Dancer-adjacent, or deep ink), one signal colour and 3–5 supporting tones.
  - Test the signal colour in the app icon at small size and in Apple's tinted and clear modes.
- **Sources:** [51], [26], [25], [19], [1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [63], [71]

### brand-archive-nostalgia — Archive-mined nostalgia
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Brands dig into their own archives (founding signage, original packaging, retired mascots, jingles) for warmth and credibility, rather than borrowing generic "retro" styling.
- **Seen in:**
  - MorningStar Farms (Sep 2026, Design Bridge and Partners): 1970s-inspired details, "Goodness Since 1974".
  - McLaren (Sep 2026): wordmark drawn from the founder's parents' service-station sign.
  - Star Wars (2026): next film's logo mimics the 1977 original.
  - Toys "R" Us (2026): brought back Geoffrey and the jingle.
  - Apple Music (Sep 2026): retro lowercase wordmark for its London venue, which commentators read as the end of minimalism.
  - [55][18][47][48][45]
- **Fits:** brands with 10+ years of history, consumer categories under pressure (MorningStar is proving plant-based "has been here for decades"), and heritage-led positioning.
- **Avoid when:** startups have no archive (don't fake one), or the original assets carry dated or exclusionary connotations.
- **Implement:** Run an archive dig for marks, type, colours, sounds and slogans. Pick one authentic artefact and redraw it for digital. Pair it with modern UI and type so it reads as confident, not costume. Tell the origin story in one line of copy.
- **Sources:** [55], [18], [47], [48], [45]

### brand-ai-agent-characters — Characters and mascots for AI agents
- **Status:** emerging
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** As AI agents ask for access to email, calendars and payments, big tech is giving them soft, customisable characters instead of abstract orbs. Characters also become long-term IP for consumer brands. Critics call this a way to defang invasive tech, and youth-safety groups are watching closely.
- **Seen in:**
  - Meta Muse (Sep 2026): plush mascot "Jolly". Fairplay said it attracts children despite the 18+ product, and marketers urged Meta to "go all in".
  - OpenAI dots (Sep 29, 2026): bubbly cartoon agent avatars.
  - Duolingo (Oct 2026): DuoTV animated series from its in-house studio.
  - Counter-example: Burger King retired "The King" to "crown the customer" (2026).
  - [41][47][40][42][43]
- **Fits:** consumer AI assistants, education, companionship and productivity apps with daily touchpoints, brands with irreverent social voices.
- **Avoid when:** adult, financial or health products, where a toy-like character can mislead or attract minors. Also enterprise buyers, and brands without the content budget to keep a character alive.
- **Implement:** Design the character as a system: expressions, states (idle, thinking, done, error), user customisation and small-size legibility. Write personality rules into the voice guide. Keep the character honest: it must not imply sentience or hide data practices. Run a minors-appeal check if the product is age-gated.
- **Sources:** [41], [47], [40], [42], [43]

### brand-system-as-code — Machine-readable brand systems (brand-as-code)
- **Status:** emerging
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Guidelines move from PDFs into machine-readable rules that AI design tools and agencies execute. Generative output is allowed, but inside brand guardrails.
- **Seen in:**
  - Coca-Cola (2025–26): Project Fizzion with Adobe encodes design intent as "StyleIDs". The 2026 identity launched with a Brand Center and "Design Intelligence" tools.
  - Typeface (2026): "Arc Graph" brand brain that structures guidelines into rules across AI channels.
  - WPP Open (2026): 35,000+ brand-consistent localised assets a year.
  - Untold (2026): AI illustration system built on art-directed guardrails.
  - [15][13][14][45][49]
- **Fits:** brands producing high volumes of localised or marketing assets, and startups using AI to generate marketing creative.
- **Avoid when:** the brand is still unsettled (encoding a weak system scales sameness). Creative leaders warn that zero-cost variation causes decision paralysis and homogeneous work.
- **Implement:**
  - Express the brand as tokens (colour, type, spacing, motion) plus written rules: voice dos and don'ts, image prompts, forbidden tropes.
  - Store it in a repo or brand portal that AI tools can read.
  - Keep a human art-director sign-off step.
  - Version it like code.
- **Sources:** [15], [13], [14], [45], [49]

### brand-ai-amplifier-positioning — AI positioned as an amplifier of people
- **Status:** mainstream
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** AI companies (and companies adding AI) position the technology as making people more capable, not replacing them. They show humans, everyday tasks and craft rather than robots, and the AI layer is framed as AI plus human expertise.
- **Seen in:**
  - Anthropic "Keep thinking" (Sep 2025, Mother): Claude for problem solvers, amplifying thinking rather than replacing it.
  - ChatGPT campaign (Sep 2025): everyday moments, real prompts.
  - Magnific (Apr 2026): "no-collar economy", creatives "more powerful than anyone expected".
  - Intuit Intelligence (Sep 2026): AI plus human experts, and clarity about whether you are talking to a person or an AI.
  - Samsara (2026): strapline "Built with", as in "people who do the work".
  - [34][33][27][22][50]
- **Fits:** AI-native products, incumbents adding agents, workforce and creative tools.
- **Avoid when:** the product really does automate a job away. Amplifier rhetoric then reads as spin, so be precise about what is automated and what remains human.
- **Implement:** Make the user the hero in headlines ("You ship…"), not the model. Show real users and outcomes. Disclose clearly when users interact with AI. Name the human-in-the-loop guarantee if there is one. Drop robot, brain and sparkle imagery.
- **Sources:** [34], [33], [27], [22], [50]

### brand-point-of-view-positioning — Point-of-view positioning for uncertain buyers
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** AI is changing products, competitors and markets at once. April Dunford argues that a point of view on where the market is going is "no longer optional": confused buyers stall, and vendors with a credible POV and a low-risk path win. The POV is not a product vision. It is your thesis about what good solutions will look like, rooted in your distinct strengths.
- **Seen in:**
  - OpenAI vs Anthropic, per Dunford's analysis (2026): "AI as metered utility" vs "AI writes the code", each matching its business.
  - Intercom (2025–26): rebuilt support around Fin, anchored on the job of resolving tickets.
  - Magnific (2026): "no-collar economy" thesis.
  - [58][60][27]
- **Fits:** B2B SaaS, AI-native startups selling to non-technical industries, and incumbents facing AI-native challengers.
- **Avoid when:** you use the POV to sell features that don't exist yet. Overselling the future stalls deals. And don't reposition after every model release: new capabilities usually strengthen an existing value theme.
- **Implement:**
  - Write a one-page POV: what is changing, what good looks like, what to do now.
  - Back it with a maturity model from today's state to the future one.
  - Keep core positioning stable, and re-run competitive alternatives, capabilities and value themes every 6 months.
  - Have founders publish the POV and back it with demos, building in public and credible early evangelists.
- **Sources:** [57], [58], [59], [60], [62]

### brand-plainspoken-outcome-voice — Plain-spoken, outcome-first verbal identity
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Verbal identities are getting shorter, warmer and more concrete: first-name-basis brand names, human taglines, and copy that leads with the outcome instead of the technology. The data is behind the shift:
  - 60% of US consumers say "AI" in brand messaging is a turnoff.
  - 50% prefer brands that avoid GenAI content.
  - Dunford: saying you have AI no longer differentiates.
- **Seen in:**
  - Kuda (Oct 2026): "Less bank, more life".
  - Samsara (2026): "Built with".
  - Novo Nordisk → "Novo" (Sep 2026): "on a first-name basis".
  - Pleo (2026): tone "credible without being cold".
  - Keen (2026, Mother Design): repositioned from "prediction" to "human guidance".
  - [53][50][52][44][26]
- **Fits:** everyone, especially SaaS homepages, AI products and fintech.
- **Avoid when:** regulated claims need precise, legally reviewed language. Plain doesn't mean vague. Also avoid forced quirkiness that every other startup already uses.
- **Implement:**
  - Replace "AI-powered X" headlines with the measurable outcome for a named customer.
  - Write voice rules as do/don't pairs with banned words ("revolutionary", "seamless", "AI-powered").
  - Test copy for LLM-legibility: state plainly what you do, for whom and against what alternatives, so AI answer engines describe you correctly. 74% of enterprise leaders rate AI discoverability a significant priority.
- **Sources:** [53], [50], [52], [44], [26], [36], [35], [61], [46]

### brand-intent-matched-store-listings — Intent-matched App Store & Google Play listings
- **Status:** rising
- **Momentum:** new
- **What:** Store positioning is now per search intent rather than one listing.
  - **App Store:**
    - Keywords can be assigned to custom product pages (CPPs) that appear in organic search (since July 2025), and the cap is now 70 per app (since Oct 29, 2025), with per-page deep links.
    - App Store tags feed "Similar Apps" placements.
    - Preview video autoplay makes the first ~10 seconds the highest-impact asset.
  - **Google Play:**
    - Keyword-targeted custom store listings, up to 50.
    - In 2026 Play Console added one-click creation from keyword recommendations and CSV-driven localisation.
- **Seen in:**
  - Apple App Store CPP keyword pages (2025–26).
  - Apple's WWDC26-announced CPP Asset Library and preview tool (fall 2026).
  - Google Play keyword custom store listings (Q2 2026 update).
  - Top ASO teams running 50–100+ tests a quarter (Phiture, 2026).
  - [64][65][66]
- **Fits:** consumer and prosumer apps with several use cases or audiences, paid-acquisition-heavy apps, and localised apps.
- **Avoid when:** you lack the creative capacity to maintain variants. Stale or misaligned pages hurt conversion, and claims must match the product to pass review.
- **Implement:**
  - Build one hero positioning plus 3–10 intent pages (by job, persona or competitor alternative), each with its own screenshot story and an outcome headline on frame 1.
  - Use real UI in every frame.
  - Cut a 10-second video opener.
  - Align screenshots with tag themes.
  - Deep-link each page to the matching in-app screen.
  - Mirror the structure on Google Play.
- **Sources:** [64], [65], [66]

## Fading — now looks dated
- **Amorphous gradient "AI orb" marks** — glowing cores, fading gradients and rotating "blossoms". The pattern is so common that LogoLounge catalogues it as a trend ("Faders"), and Fast Company mocked 2025's AI "butthole" logos. It no longer differentiates an AI product. [6][9]
- **"AI-powered" as the headline** — audiences discount it and buyers ask for outcomes. [35][36][61]
- **Undisclosed, fully AI-generated campaign visuals** — McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas ad within days (Dec 2025), and Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ads drew backlash. [39][11]
- **Equity-erasing "blanding"** — Cracker Barrel's plain wordmark was reversed within about a week (2025). McLaren's quieter wordmark was criticised as bland (2026). Apple Music's colourless black-and-white icon (iOS 27.2 beta 4, Oct 13) drew immediate backlash, and Creative Bloq asked "I thought we were done with minimalism?" (Oct 2026). [21][7][56][67]
- **Default tech blue and neon SaaS gradients** — Samsara swapped tech blue for construction yellow, Calendly replaced neon with pastels, and Pleo sets its pink against category greys and blues. [51][25][26]
- **Generic challenger-fintech kit** (geometric sans, flat vector people, teal) — Kuda moved "on from its challenger days" with hand-drawn illustration. [53]
- **Static PDF brand books** — Coca-Cola's Fizzion was premised on rules machines can't read; guidelines are becoming StyleIDs and brand brains. [15][14][45]
- **Baked-in skeuomorphic icon effects and realistic 3D icon objects** — Apple's glass system asks for flat, frontal, layered artwork, and drop shadows and bevels now duplicate the system material. [63]
